02 · The machinery

The Grievance Loop

What it looks like from the outside
Withdrawal.
Attacking.
Victimhood.
Martyrdom.
Complaining.
Blaming.
Passive aggression.

Seven faces of the same machinery. From the inside, each one feels justified. That’s recognition, not blame. The Grievance Loop is the first of three this program walks through.

Left alone, one person’s loop becomes the team’s weather.

Not a defect — equipment

Grievance is human, not personal.

The capacity for grievance comes standard. Most people run it quietly and call it stress, or principle, or high standards. The person this page is about runs it louder — that’s the only difference.

Which makes it workable. If it were personality, there'd be nothing to fix. A mechanism can be seen, released, and rebuilt — first in them, then by them.

03 · The neutralizing

The Release Loop

The first move is zooming out.

The work starts where the person is: their current model, exactly as it comes. What are you pissed off about? Mom, dad, brother, drivers on the road, the boss from twenty-five years back — it all comes out. No bad parts. The machinery is working perfectly, exactly as designed. It just hasn’t been directed yet.

Then the zoom-out: you see the loop as a loop — something you’ve been sitting in, not who you are. From there it gets neutralized: no new suffering, and the suffering it already caused gets cleared. Release as in released: an unburdening.

What it sounds like from inside the work

04 · The harness

The Empowered Loop

But wait — there’s more. The fire doesn’t get put out. It gets harnessed.

The same power inside the grievance becomes a loop the person builds and owns. Grinding becomes problem-solving; prosecuting becomes understanding. Same energy, aimed at the work instead of the wound.

It runs in two directions: at yourself and at the people around you.

Aimed at yourself

The same machinery turns inward: spotting where your own model doesn't match the evidence in front of you — and updating it instead of defending it. What's left after a release is a mismatch of models. That can become a productive dialogue instead of a cold war.

Aimed at someone else

When someone comes at you — attacking, withdrawing, going strange — the first question is: “What rule do you have that I broke?” Run the empathy interview. Put your evidence on the table. Help them release the grievance they’re holding against you.

06

What This Isn’t

Not therapy

No excavating childhood. No sitting with feelings until they soften. The work is structural: find the mechanism, watch it run, change it. Anyone who needs clinical care should have it — this is a different instrument.

Not coaching on retainer

Seven sessions, then it ends. No open-ended hourly relationship. No dependency by design.

Not performance management

Nothing from the sessions is reported back to you. This isn’t a paper trail, and it shouldn’t be sold to the employee as one.

Not a personality transplant

The goal was never to make someone agreeable. Disagreement is useful. Rehearsed resentment isn’t.

07 · Why us

Where This Comes From

JOurneY is the work of Bijoy Goswami and Aviri, built over twenty-five years around a single question: what model is this person actually running?

People don’t behave randomly. They run models — of how work should go, what respect looks like, where the line is. Most were installed early and never examined. When a model stops matching reality, it doesn’t announce itself. It shows up as friction.

Grievance is where that becomes visible, so it's the entry point. The method is the one Aviri has run with founders, teams, and communities for twenty-five years — run on the participant's behalf, not taught. They don't need a framework; they need the loop to stop.

His favorite telling is the founding itself: the Declaration was a grievance loop, and it won independence. The first model after it threw the baby out with the bathwater. What finally worked was the Empowered Loop — iterate the model, update it, keep updating it until the thing holds. Marbury v. Madison is how he explains jurisdiction inside the course.

09 · What happens

Seven sessions across seven weeks. A follow-up three weeks later.

A prep week, seven working sessions, an end you can point to. The first stretch is recognition: seeing the Grievance Loop that has been running them, sometimes for years. The middle neutralizes it into the Release Loop and clears what has accumulated. The last sessions build the Empowered Loop and rehearse it, so there’s something to run when the next trigger arrives. Then three weeks of practice in the world, ten minutes of journaling a day, and a review of how it's holding.

What it’s like for them

Nobody arrives in a good mood, and that's planned for. We don’t ask anyone to be more positive or ready to grow. We start with the grievances exactly as they are, treated as legitimate, because they usually are.

What changes is that they see the machinery from the outside for the first time. Most people describe the weight coming off before they can describe what happened.

Work and personal grievances both come along — these patterns rarely started at the office. It stays confidential either way.
