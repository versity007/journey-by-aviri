The Cost Isn’t One Difficult Person. It’s Everyone Working Around Them.

Seven weeks, one-on-one: a journey through the three loops that run a grievance — and out the other side.

JOurneY is a confidential program for a single employee — the talented one whose friction has started shaping how everyone else behaves. It walks them through the three loops of a grievance: the one that’s running them now, the one that releases it, and the one they build to replace it.

Not therapy. Not coaching on retainer. A fixed container with a beginning, a middle, and an end.

Right fit

A valuable person whose negativity is now affecting the team, after feedback has already been tried.

Wrong fit

A team workshop, a performance-management paper trail, or someone who needs clinical care.

Left alone, one person’s loop becomes the team’s weather.

Not a defect — equipment

Grievance is human, not personal.

The capacity for grievance comes standard. Everyone on your team has it; so do you. Most people run it quietly and call it stress, or principle, or high standards. The person this page is about runs it louder — that’s the only difference.

Which makes it workable. If it were their personality, there would be nothing to fix. A mechanism everyone carries can be seen, released, and rebuilt — first in them, and later, by them.

The neutralizing

The Release Loop

The first move is zooming out.

The work starts where the person is: their current model, exactly as it comes. What are you pissed off about? Mom, dad, brother, drivers on the road, the boss from twenty-five years back — it all comes out. No bad parts: the machinery is working perfectly, exactly as designed. It just hasn’t been directed yet.

Then the zoom-out: you see the loop as a loop — something you’ve been sitting in, not who you are. From there it gets neutralized: no new suffering, and the suffering it already caused gets cleared. Released from the loop itself. Release as in released: an unburdening.

The harness

The Empowered Loop

But wait — there’s more. The fire doesn’t get put out. It gets harnessed.

The same power that was inside the grievance becomes a loop the person builds and owns. Grinding becomes problem-solving; prosecuting becomes understanding. Same energy, aimed at the work instead of the wound.

That’s the gift hidden inside a grievance: the energy prosecuting the wound was never the enemy. Released, it becomes the person’s most productive asset — built and owned by them, not installed by us.

It runs in two directions — aimed at the people around you, and aimed at yourself.

Aimed at someone else

When someone comes at you — attacking, withdrawing, going strange — the first question is: “What rule do you have that I broke?” Maybe you did break it. Maybe it’s a rule you didn’t know existed. Maybe they made up a story about what you were doing, and the reality is something else. You run the empathy interview, put your evidence on the table, and help them release the grievance they’ve been holding against you.

Aimed at yourself

The same machinery turns inward: spotting where your own model of reality doesn’t match the evidence in front of you — and updating the model instead of defending it. What’s left after a release is usually a mismatch of models between two people. That can finally become a productive dialogue instead of a cold war.

What This Isn’t

Not therapy

We’re not excavating childhood or sitting with feelings until they soften. The work is structural: find the mechanism, watch it run, change it. Anyone who needs clinical care should have it — this is a different instrument.

Not coaching on retainer

Seven sessions, then it ends. No open-ended hourly relationship and no dependency by design.

Not performance management

Nothing from the sessions is reported back to you. This isn’t a paper trail, and it shouldn’t be sold to the employee as one.

Not a personality transplant

The goal was never to make someone agreeable. Disagreement is useful. Rehearsed resentment isn’t.

Why us

Where This Comes From

JOurneY is the work of Bijoy Goswami and Aviri, built over twenty-five years of a single question: what model is this person actually running?

People don’t behave randomly. They run models — of how work should go, what respect looks like, where the line is. Most were installed early and never examined. When a model stops matching reality, it doesn’t announce itself; it shows up as friction.

Grievance is where that becomes visible, which is why it’s the entry point. The method behind the sessions is the same one Aviri has used with founders, teams, and communities for twenty-five years. We run it on the participant’s behalf rather than teaching it — they don’t need a framework, they need the loop to stop.

The Experience

Seven sessions across seven weeks. A follow-up three weeks later.

Seven weeks is a sprint, not a subscription: a prep week, seven working sessions, and an end you can point to. The first stretch is recognition — seeing the Grievance Loop that has been running them, sometimes for years. The middle neutralizes it into the Release Loop and clears what has accumulated. The last sessions build the Empowered Loop and rehearse it, so there’s something to run when the next trigger arrives. Then they practice in the world for three weeks, ten minutes of journaling a day, and we review how it’s holding.

What it’s like for them

Nobody arrives here in a good mood about it, and that’s planned for. We don’t ask anyone to be more positive or ready to grow. We start with the grievances exactly as they are, treated as legitimate, because they usually are.

What changes is that they see the machinery from the outside for the first time. Most people describe the weight coming off before they can describe what happened.

Work and personal grievances both come along — these patterns rarely started at the office — and it stays confidential either way.
